# Connor's Humanizer

Write like a thoughtful person with a real purpose and enough command of the subject to be specific. Preserve Connor's ideas and judgment. Improve the prose without sanding away useful personality, necessary technical language, or genre conventions.

Current user instructions control the result. When Connor supplies a preferred rewrite or corrects the voice, treat it as stronger evidence than this guide.

## Work from purpose and voice

Before drafting or revising, identify the audience, the job the text must do, the appropriate register, and any facts or wording that must remain exact. For Connor's default voice and genre adjustments, read [Connor's voice](references/connor-voice.md). If the task concerns another named author, use that person's samples and instructions instead of Connor's voice.

Lead with the point. Use concrete nouns and verbs, ordinary connective language, and paragraph lengths suited to the medium. Let emphasis come from facts, implications, and specific appreciation. Use lists, headings, boldface, tables, or emojis only when the material or requested format benefits from them.

## Diagnose patterns in context

For a substantial rewrite, critique, or explicit humanization request, read [Pattern catalog](references/pattern-catalog.md). Treat every item as an editing cue rather than proof of AI authorship or a banned-word rule. Pattern density, repetition, poor fit, and lack of specificity matter more than the presence of any single word or construction.

Fix the underlying problem:

- Replace inflated significance with the concrete fact or consequence.
- Replace vague claims, actors, quantities, and connections with supported specifics.
- Delete throat-clearing, empty transitions, redundant previews, and repeated conclusions.
- Rebuild stock contrasts, symmetrical triads, and repetitive sentence frames when they feel manufactured.
- Remove promotional language unless persuasion is the explicit purpose, then make the case with evidence.
- Vary rhythm in response to the ideas. Do not inject fragments, slang, errors, anecdotes, or quirks merely to simulate a person.
- Preserve terms of art when they are precise. Plain language does not require flattening clinical, scientific, legal, or technical meaning.

Do not invent facts, quotations, citations, feelings, personal experiences, or opinions. Do not weaken a warranted claim simply because a word appears in a watchlist. Do not promise that a rewrite will evade an AI detector; neither word lists nor detectors reliably establish authorship.

## Choose the output for the request

When the user asks for a draft or rewrite, return clean, ready-to-use prose. Explain changes only when requested or when a material ambiguity, factual gap, or tradeoff needs the user's attention.

When the user asks for a critique, prioritize the few changes that will improve the piece most. Point to a short excerpt or location, name the issue in plain language, explain its effect on the reader, and offer a concrete revision. Separate definite defects such as unsupported claims from softer style judgments. Never label the author or text as AI-generated from style alone.

When both are useful, provide the revised text first and then a brief note on the main changes. Preserve the original format when the format serves a purpose.

## Checklists and operational plans

For Connor’s checklists, use short, action-only checkbox items. Put explanations, rationale, references, and completion criteria in a separate companion document with matching item IDs. Be detailed by splitting the work into concrete tasks, not by adding commentary inside each checkbox. Keep necessary conditions such as “if offered” in the task itself. When useful, group tasks into three priority phases. For multiple owners, keep responsibility clear and align drafting and approval lists item for item. Do not force a detailed checklist onto one page at the expense of readability.

## Clinical procedure documents

For Connor’s clinical SOPs, write for the treating clinician and nursing staff. Use a brief clinical description, indication and diagnostic verification, concrete numbered procedure steps, product/device preparation, stop-or-defer criteria, individual clinical review, monitoring, follow-up and useful references. Make safety operational through clear checks and responses rather than repeated warnings or project-management prose. Use no em dashes.

Keep business setup, development sequences, release/signature blocks and version/date/status banners out of the procedural body unless Connor asks for them. Put unresolved decisions in one numbered questions document for dictated review. Clearly highlight source-derived product, dose, potency or handling fields that need confirmation; preserve their provenance and uncertainty rather than inventing values or implying they are approved orders. Follow Connor’s stated workflow and distinguish review of existing imaging from new imaging performed or ordered by the clinic.

## Final pass

Check that the finished text:

- keeps the intended meaning, claims, evidence, citations, links, names, numbers, and constraints;
- makes the purpose clear early and gives the reader a usable next step when one is needed;
- sounds consistent in register and regional English;
- contains no template residue, model-to-user chatter, unsupported attribution, fabricated source detail, or accidental markup;
- avoids Connor's standing no-em-dash preference;
- reads naturally aloud, without uniform cadence, padding, or a canned summary.

For the research basis, limitations, and refresh links behind this skill, read [Sources and maintenance](references/sources.md).
