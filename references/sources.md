# Sources and maintenance

Reviewed October 12, 2026. These links support the skill's diagnostic categories and editing method. They do not establish authorship of any individual text.

## Primary field guide

- [Wikipedia: Signs of AI writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing) is the main pattern inventory. It covers content habits, vocabulary clusters, sentence constructions, formatting, model-to-user residue, markup, citations, and historical indicators. Wikipedia labels it an advice page rather than policy and warns that the observations are descriptive, context-dependent, and insufficient on their own to prove AI use. Some entries are specific to Wikipedia and are applied here only when they generalize to prose or artifact integrity.

## Model and platform guidance

- [Anthropic: Prompting best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices) recommends direct, grounded communication and shows how positive instructions can reduce excessive Markdown and fragmented bulleting. It also notes that prompt format can influence response format.
- [Anthropic: The new rules of context engineering for Claude 5 generation models](https://claude.dev/blog/the-new-rules-of-context-engineering-for-claude-5-generation-models/) argues for concise, judgment-based instructions and progressive disclosure instead of sprawling universal rules. This shaped the skill's short entrypoint and conditional pattern reference.
- [Microsoft: How to humanize AI text for natural writing](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-copilot/copilot-101/humanize-ai-text) recommends audience-specific tone, concrete vocabulary, sentence variation driven by flow, and reading aloud. It identifies vagueness, repeated structures, redundant phrasing, neutral tone, and excessive dashes as common editing targets.

## Research and editorial cross-checks

- [Geng and Trotta, Human-LLM Coevolution: Evidence from Academic Writing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09606) tracks changing word frequencies in arXiv abstracts. It supports treating lexical signatures as model- and time-dependent rather than permanent banned-word lists, and it discusses the limits of individual text detection.
- [Humanetext: 15 Words and Phrases That Give Away AI Writing](https://humanetext.com/blog/15-words-phrases-that-give-away-ai-writing) is a practitioner editing guide that emphasizes density, specificity, deletion of filler, and the legitimate uses of flagged words. It is used as an editorial cross-check, not as scientific proof.
- [Tracy Sheen: AI Words to Avoid](https://tracysheen.com/resources/ai-words-to-avoid) is a maintained practitioner list. Its useful principle is that clusters and poor fit matter more than a word's presence, and that an author's genuine vocabulary should be preserved. Its blanket-ban prompt is intentionally not adopted here.

## Refresh method

When this skill produces repeated false positives, misses a recurring pattern, or begins to feel dated, refresh it rather than continually adding prohibitions:

1. Re-read Wikipedia's current caveats, contents, language, style, communication, and historical-indicator sections.
2. Check current official model-writing guidance from Anthropic and OpenAI when model behavior is relevant.
3. Prefer corpus studies and documented editing outcomes over viral prompt lists.
4. Update categories and decision criteria. Keep specific words as watch items and preserve legitimate genre-specific uses.
5. Incorporate Connor's direct corrections into the voice reference when they recur across tasks.
